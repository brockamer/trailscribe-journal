This was a point-to-point drive from San Gabriel, in the western San Gabriel Valley, to Malibu on the coast. The log closed at 56.11 miles after 163 minutes behind the wheel, crossing the Los Angeles Basin from the foothill suburbs to the edge of the Pacific.

Conditions could hardly have been kinder. The air sat at 72°F with a 5 mph breeze and clear skies the whole way. Visibility of that kind is not guaranteed in the basin, and the day felt more like a long scenic transit than a commute.

The terrain barely registered. Total elevation gain came to just 180 feet over the full distance, so the drive stayed on the floor of the basin before easing toward sea level. Any climbing came as gentle undulations rather than real grades.

Pace was the defining feature of the session. The recorded average speed was 29.5 mph, while the 95th-percentile speed reached 57.1 mph. That spread is typical of a metro crossing. Open stretches let the car run near highway speed, and slower segments pulled the overall figure well below it. The 163-minute duration reflects that stop-and-go character more than the mileage does.

The trip ended in Malibu, where the urban grid gives way to the shoreline. It made a quiet, flat, sun-washed finish to a long valley-to-coast run under an open sky.
