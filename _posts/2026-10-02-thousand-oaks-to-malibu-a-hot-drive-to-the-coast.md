Clear sky, warm wind blows

A short point-to-point drive carried this session from Thousand Oaks south to Malibu. It crossed the 12.11 miles between the inland Conejo Valley and the Pacific shore in 31 minutes. The route links two very different settings: the oak-dotted suburbs and dry hills of Thousand Oaks on one end, and the coastal edge of Malibu on the other, with the Santa Monica Mountains rising between them.

Conditions were hot and bright. The thermometer read 94°F under a clear sky, with wind at about 14 mph. That is typical late-season heat for this side of the range, when the hills turn gold and the air holds little moisture. At those temperatures, the drop toward the ocean usually brings the most noticeable change of the trip.

The drive logged 871 feet of elevation gain, which reflects the rise and fall needed to get over the mountains rather than a steady descent to sea level. The average speed was 33.4 mph, with a 95th-percentile speed of 53.8 mph. That pattern points to a mix of open stretches and slower winding sections, which fits a mountain crossing where curves, grades, and traffic set the pace more than the posted limits do.

There were no long stops in the record, so the trip reads as one continuous run from start to finish, a little over half an hour from the valley to the coast.
