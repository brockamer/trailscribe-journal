Left the trailhead under a cloudless Malibu sky, temperature already sitting at 72°F with a light 4 mph onshore breeze that did just enough to keep the sweat from stacking up. No elevation gain across the full 3.75 miles, which on the surface reads as easy — but flat coastal terrain in Malibu has its own particular character. The ground is sun-hardened, the scrub presses close on both sides of the track, and the light comes in at a low coastal angle that flattens shadows and makes distance judgment tricky.

The pace out was steady. Overall average held at 4.4 mph for the session, though the p95 touching 7.6 mph tells the story of at least one stretch where the footing opened up and the legs found a different gear — likely along a wider fire-road section where the surface was compacted and the sight lines extended. Out-and-back routes in this part of Malibu tend to have that rhythm: tighter brush corridor on the approach, then a more exposed return leg where you're moving into the view rather than away from it.

Stopped once at roughly the turnaround, maybe a minute or two, just to mark the conditions. The breeze off the water was more noticeable here, carrying that particular mix of dry chaparral and ocean salt that defines the Malibu backcountry in a way no other coastal range quite replicates. No marine layer today — the sky stayed hard blue the entire time, which meant full sun exposure on the return leg.

Total time on trail: 66 minutes. No gain to recover from, no technical sections to account for. The session reads as a maintenance effort or a deliberate easy day — the kind of outing where you're not pushing the body but keeping the legs honest and staying familiar with the terrain. Malibu's flat coastal tracks reward that kind of regular, unhurried attention.
