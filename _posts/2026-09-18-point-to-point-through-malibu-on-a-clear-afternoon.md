Left the trailhead with 68°F air sitting flat and easy against the skin, a 2 mph onshore drift barely registering as wind. The sky was unbroken — no marine layer, no haze — just the kind of hard coastal blue that makes distances look shorter than they are.

The route ran point-to-point across 2.65 miles of Malibu terrain, gaining only 47 feet of elevation over the full stretch. That number tells the story plainly: this is lateral country, not vertical. The land rolls in low, unhurried swells, chaparral pressing close on either side of the path, dry stems ticking against boot leather at the margins.

Pace averaged 4.0 mph across the hour, which is a working clip for this kind of ground — attentive but not labored. The p95 speed touching 7.0 mph suggests at least one open, firm section where the trail widened and the footing was clean enough to push. That stretch felt like the middle third of the route, where the scrub pulled back and the sight lines opened toward the ridgeline to the north.

A long pause somewhere past the midpoint — the kind of stop that doesn't show up in the average but registers in the legs afterward. The stillness at that moment was complete. No traffic noise reached this far in. A scrub jay moved through the brush without urgency. The soil here was pale and powdery, cracked in the fine reticulated pattern that follows a dry stretch, and the tracks preserved in it were shallow but legible: deer, likely from the previous evening given the edge degradation on the prints.

The final half mile back toward the Malibu end ran slightly downhill by feel, though the elevation profile barely confirms it — 47 feet total gain on a point-to-point means the net change is nearly flat, and whatever descent existed was gentle enough to feel like relief rather than effort. Finished in 60 minutes with legs still under me and the light unchanged from when I started, the sun having moved only a short distance across that clean, cloudless sky.
