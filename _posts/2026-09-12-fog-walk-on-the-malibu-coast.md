Fog erases cliffs  
salt air heavy, slow footsteps  
the shore finds itself

Left the trailhead just under a mile out and back, the route running flat along the Malibu shoreline with only 60 feet of elevation gain across the full 1.91 miles. The fog had settled in hard by the time I started, visibility cut to maybe a quarter mile, the Pacific reduced to a gray suggestion beyond the wet sand. Temperature held at 69°F with a 4 mph onshore breeze — cool enough to feel the moisture on exposed skin but not uncomfortable.

Pace came in at an average of 4.1 mph, though the 95th-percentile speed of 3.1 mph tells the more honest story: most of the walk was slower, deliberate, with the faster average pulled up by a few open stretches where the path was clear and the footing firm. The fog does something to pace — it compresses the visible world and there is less urgency to move through it quickly.

Stopped at the turnaround point for several minutes. The ocean noise was the dominant presence: low, continuous, directional in a way that fog makes strange. Without a clear horizon the sound seemed to come from everywhere at once. A few shorebirds worked the tideline but stayed low, shapes more than birds in the murk.

The return leg covered the same ground in reverse, the fog unchanged, the 4 mph wind still pushing inland. Total time on foot was 47 minutes. No sun, no shadows, no hard edges anywhere — just the muted palette of a Malibu morning swallowed whole by marine layer.
